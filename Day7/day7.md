# 🧭 My Claude Usage Strategy

**Profile:** Student · Daily user · Needs: Learning Support, Coding Help, Deep Research, Creative Work
**Context:** Building web dev + Python + marketing skills, running a 60-Day AI Mastery content series, prepping for job/internship applications on a 3-month timeline.

---

## Recommended Primary Model
### 🟣 Claude Sonnet 5

For a daily-use student juggling coding, learning, research, and content creation, **Sonnet 5** is your workhorse model. It's fast enough for daily back-and-forth, smart enough for real coding and research work, and far more cost/usage-efficient than Opus for high-frequency use.

---

## Why This Model Fits Me

| Your Reality | Why Sonnet 5 Matches |
|---|---|
| Daily, high-frequency use | Sonnet burns through usage limits far slower than Opus |
| Mixed workload (code + writing + research) | Sonnet 5 handles all of these well without needing a model switch every time |
| 3-month deadline, need speed + iteration | Sonnet responds quickly, good for "build in public" pace |
| Deep research is only *sometimes* needed | Save Opus for those specific heavy-lift moments, not everyday use |

---

## When to Use Haiku
Use **Claude Haiku 4.5** for:
- Quick fact lookups, syntax reminders, definitions
- Rewriting a sentence, shortening a caption, fixing a typo
- Fast iteration when you're bulk-generating small content pieces (e.g., 10 LinkedIn post hooks to skim through)
- Anything where you'd rather have an answer in 2 seconds than a perfect one in 20

**Rule of thumb:** if you already basically know the answer and just want it confirmed fast — Haiku.

---

## When to Use Sonnet
Use **Claude Sonnet 5** (your daily driver) for:
- Coding help — debugging React/Next.js, writing REST API calls, SQL queries, Git workflows
- Learning support — explaining a new concept, walking through a tutorial, practicing interview questions
- Drafting LinkedIn posts, project descriptions, portfolio copy
- Day-to-day research (e.g., "what's the current best practice for X")
- Resume bullets, cover letters, outreach messages

**Rule of thumb:** this is your default. Only leave it when a task is either trivially easy (→ Haiku) or genuinely high-stakes/complex (→ Opus).

---

## When to Use Opus
Use **Claude Opus 4.8** for:
- Deep, multi-source research where accuracy and thoroughness really matter (e.g., researching a niche job market, comparing multiple frameworks in depth before committing months to one)
- High-stakes writing: your actual resume final draft, a cover letter for a dream role, a business/portfolio strategy doc
- Complex, multi-step coding problems Sonnet struggles with (architecture decisions, tricky bugs across a large codebase)
- Planning your 12-week roadmap or making structural decisions about your career strategy

**Rule of thumb:** reach for Opus when being *wrong* or *shallow* actually costs you something real — not for routine work.

---

## Recommended Effort Level

### Low
Quick facts, simple rewrites, one-line answers. Rare for you — most of your work needs at least some reasoning.

### Standard *(your default — use most often)*
Almost everything: coding help, learning explanations, content drafts, day-to-day research. This is where you should live 80% of the time.

### High
- Debugging a tricky piece of code
- Researching something you'll act on (e.g., which stack to learn next)
- Reviewing/polishing a deliverable before it goes public or gets submitted

### Max
Reserve for genuinely high-stakes, one-shot-needs-to-be-right moments:
- Final resume/cover letter before submitting to a real application
- A big architectural decision for your portfolio project
- Mock interview prep for an actual upcoming interview
- Deep research that will shape weeks of your roadmap

---

## Biggest Mistakes I Should Avoid

1. **Using Opus for everyday coding/learning tasks** — burns through usage fast for no real quality gain on routine work.
2. **Using Haiku for anything you'll actually submit or publish** — fine for drafts, risky for final output.
3. **Staying at Standard effort for high-stakes moments** — your final resume, real interview prep, and big strategic decisions deserve High or Max.
4. **Treating "deep research" as one mode** — quick research (Sonnet, Standard) and decision-shaping research (Opus, High) are different jobs; don't underpower the second one.
5. **Not batching similar tasks** — jumping between models mid-task wastes time; pick the model for the *session*, not the message.

---

## Final Recommendation

> **If you could use only ONE model and ONE effort level for most of your work:**
>
> **Claude Sonnet 5 at Standard effort.**
>
> As a daily user balancing coding, learning, content creation, and research on a 3-month timeline, this combination gives you the best coverage: fast enough to keep pace with daily use, capable enough for real coding and writing work, and efficient enough that you won't hit usage limits mid-week. Reserve Opus + High/Max as a *deliberate switch* for the handful of moments each week that are genuinely high-stakes — your final resume, a real interview, or a big strategic decision. Everything else stays in Sonnet 5 / Standard.
